What is a Radar?

The concept behind a radar is quite simple. It is very similiar to how bats find their surroundings using echo-location. But in this case, we are just using radio waves instead of sound waves. The radar sends out a radio wave pulse, and then listens to how much of that pulse got reflected back. Now, different materials send back more or less of the wave. Light rain, bugs, and even wood has a low "reflectivity" (the amount of the radio wave that got reflected back, which I will refer to it as), whereas things like metal, buildings, and the ground have a high "reflectivity".

The frequency's

A radar's frequency is very important, as its tied to both the range, and the resolution of the data. There are different frequencies that are commonly used, and are often called "bands". There are many different bands, but in meteorology, we typically only use a few, which names are based on the IEEE standard. Modern weather radars use the S band, where older radars typically use a C band. There are also smaller radars, like the ones used by Climavision, or mobile radars like the DOW's (all 8) which use X band radars. The Longer the wave bands typically have a longer range, but have a lower resolution, where as shorter bands have a higher resolution, but a lower range, due to the wave frequency being higher, causing it to be absorbed alot more.
